How this works / limitations

This app runs Meta's open-source HT-Demucs music source-separation model locally via the official demucs Python package. Your audio file is processed entirely on this machine and never leaves it.

Two models are available: htdemucs_6s (6 stems, adds Guitar/Piano) and htdemucs_ft (4 stems, no Guitar/Piano). Demucs' own authors document htdemucs_6s as measurably lower-fidelity than htdemucs_ft — splitting out Guitar and Piano costs separation quality on every stem, not just those two. If you don't need Guitar/Piano isolated, pick "Best Quality" above for noticeably cleaner Drums/Bass/Vocals/Other.

The extracted vocals stem is then optionally run through a second AI pass — a community "Karaoke" model from the UVR (Ultimate Vocal Remover) project — to split it further into Lead Vocals and Backing Vocals (harmonies/BGVs).

No publicly available open model distinguishes lead vs rhythm guitar, or treats percussion and keyboards as fully separate stems from drums/piano — those finer splits don't exist outside of proprietary commercial services (e.g. Moises.ai's paid API), which this app doesn't use since everything here runs locally and free. HT-Demucs's remaining outputs (drums, bass, other, guitar, piano) are the finest breakdown available locally, so percussion is folded into Drums and lead/rhythm guitar come out as one combined Guitar stem.

The first time you use each model, the app downloads it and caches it, so later runs skip the download: htdemucs_6s is roughly 300–500 MB, and htdemucs_ft (a bag of 4 models) is larger, around 1 GB. The first time you request Lead/Backing Vocals it also downloads the karaoke model (a few hundred MB more). Processing speed depends on your CPU/GPU and the quality settings above — expect anywhere from under a minute to several minutes per song.
